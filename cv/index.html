Experience

  • 2026 - Present
    Undergraduate Researcher (Project Lead)
    Uhler Lab (PI: Caroline Uhler)
    Representation Learning of Single-Cell Images
    • Developed a self-supervised framework that separates perturbation-driven morphology from plate-to-plate technical variation; trained on 4.3M cells across 3,030 CRISPR gene knockouts
    • Improved cross-plate replicate retrieval ~7× over DINOv2 and ~9× over Cell-DINO (mAP 0.0036 and 0.0027 → 0.0249) on held-out genes, and raised the share of knockouts above AUC 0.6 (22% vs 5–6% for the baselines)
    • Confirmed the metrics were consistent on held-out genes from a function-disjoint split, suggesting generalization
  • 2026 - Present
    Undergraduate Researcher (Project Lead)
    Atomic Architects Lab (PI: Tess Smidt)
    FrOGS: Discrete Neural Sampler Across Chemical Conditions
    • Conceived and developed a discrete neural sampler that couples an autoregressive model to a learned continuous-time Markov chain, generating independent configurations from the Boltzmann distribution across chemical conditions
    • Matched exact free energies of the 2D Ising model on a 20×20 lattice to within 0.05% across its critical temperature
    • Recovered all 3 CuAu ordered phases, whereas a published neural baseline missed CuAu₃, a thermodynamically delicate phase just below the convex hull of the free energy
    • Used as a baseline by researchers at Microsoft Research and the University of Cambridge (He et al., arXiv:2610.01739)
  • 2025 - 2026
    Undergraduate Researcher (Project Lead)
    Quantum Coherent Electronics Lab (PI: Kevin O'Brien)
    Pulse Sequence Compiler for FPGA-Controlled Superconducting Qubit Experiments
    • Developed a pulse sequence compiler integrating Fermilab’s Quantum Instrumentation Control Kit (QICK) into MIT’s internal Python quantum framework (QCML), translating multi-channel experiment definitions and parameter sweeps into FPGA-native programs for qubit control and readout
    • Demonstrated an end-to-end superconducting qubit characterization with the compiler, including T₁ and T₂ measurements
    • Adopted by a team of 4 researchers at MIT Lincoln Lab for further development including multiplexing and active reset
  • 2024 - 2026
    Undergraduate Researcher
    Atomic Architects Lab (PI: Tess Smidt)
    Learning Lattice Parameters from Powder X-Ray Diffraction Data Using Invariants
    • Designed a transformer encoder architecture and trained it on the MP-20 dataset for bispectrum (an invariant lattice geometry descriptor) prediction from XRD patterns
    • Showed that bispectrum intermediate representations outperform direct prediction for lattice parameter reconstruction from XRD data, reducing the mean absolute percentage error of cell lengths by ~2× on the experimental RRUFF dataset
